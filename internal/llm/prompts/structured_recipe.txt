Unit rules — follow strictly:
- Liquids (oil, soy sauce, vinegar, stock, juice): use ml or g, never tablespoons/teaspoons/cups.
- Dry solids (flour, sugar, spices, cheese, vegetables, meat): use g or kg.
- Small spice amounts under 5 g: still use g (e.g. '2 g salt', '1 g pepper').
- Genuinely countable whole items where weight is impractical: use a plain integer with no unit (e.g. '2 eggs', '4 chicken thighs', '30 gyoza wrappers'). Set unit to null.
- Never use 'pieces', 'cloves', 'tablespoons', 'teaspoons', 'cups', 'oz', 'lb', 'fl oz'.
- Garlic: convert cloves to grams (1 clove ≈ 5 g).
- Non-numeric amounts like 'to taste' or 'for garnish': set quantity and unit to null, put the phrase in quantity_text.
- Temperatures are always °C, converted from °F where the source is American (350°F -> 175°C, 400°F -> 200°C), rounded to the nearest 5. Gas marks become °C too.
- The step text follows the same rules as the ingredient list: metric only, never cups, tablespoons, teaspoons, oz, lb, inches. Pan and tin sizes in cm.

Time rules — follow strictly:
- total_minutes is the real elapsed time from starting the recipe until the food is ready to eat: prep, cook, and any waiting (marinating, proofing, resting, chilling, cooling) all count toward it. It is NOT simply prep_minutes + cook_minutes — when prep happens while something else cooks or waits, or when there's waiting time that is neither hands-on prep nor active cooking, total_minutes reflects the true wall-clock time and can be less than or greater than that sum.
- prep_minutes is hands-on active time only: chopping, mixing, assembling, kneading. Do not include passive waiting (marinating, proofing, resting, chilling) in prep_minutes even if it happens before cooking starts.
- cook_minutes is time the food is actively being heated: on the stovetop, in the oven, simmering, baking, frying — including passive heating time like unattended baking or simmering. Do not include unheated waiting (marinating, proofing, resting, chilling, cooling) in cook_minutes.
- If the source explicitly states a total time, prep time, or cook time (e.g. a recipe card, page metadata, or a sentence in the text), prefer that stated value over your own step-by-step estimate, unless it's clearly inconsistent with the steps. Otherwise, estimate all three from the steps' text and timer_seconds.

Step granularity rules — follow strictly:
- A step is one thing a person stands at the counter and does, not one atomic action. Someone cooking reads a step, does it, and looks up again — a step they finish in under a minute was never worth looking up for.
- Merge short consecutive actions in the same place into the step they belong to: "heat the oil, add the onion and garlic, and soften for 5 minutes" is ONE step, not three. "Add the salt" is never a step of its own.
- Only split an action into its own step when it earns one: it has its own timer, it belongs to a different phase, it runs parallel to something else, or it is a genuine decision point the cook must not miss.
- As a guide, a 30-minute recipe is about 5 to 8 steps, not 15. Prefer fewer, fuller steps over many thin ones.
- timer_seconds is the real duration of that step — never set a timer under 60 seconds unless the recipe truly hinges on those seconds (searing, blanching, blooming spices).

Step phase rule — set each step's phase to one of:
- "prep" — mise en place: chopping, measuring, mixing, marinade assembly, anything done before or alongside the heat that is hands-on preparation.
- "cook" — the heat is on and the food is being cooked: stovetop, oven, frying, simmering, baking.
- "wait" — passive time where the cook does nothing: marinating, proofing, resting, chilling, cooling. Waiting for the oven to preheat or a pot to come to the boil is "wait" only when nothing else is happening; if there is prep to do alongside it, see the parallelism rule.
This is the same three-way split as prep_minutes / cook_minutes / the waiting time inside total_minutes — keep them consistent.

Parallelism rule — this is how a real cook works, so get it right:
- Give consecutive steps that happen AT THE SAME TIME the same parallel_group integer (1, 2, 3, … counting up through the recipe). Steps that run alone get null.
- The case to look for: a long step that does not need hands — water coming to the boil, the oven preheating, pasta cooking, a sauce reducing, something roasting — with hands-on work that fits inside it. Put both in one group: "bring a large pot of salted water to the boil" and "meanwhile, dice the onion and pepper" are one group, not two steps in sequence.
- Only group steps that genuinely can overlap. If a step needs the previous one finished (you cannot sauce the pasta before it is drained), leave parallel_group null.
- Write each step in a group so it stands on its own — do not start one with "meanwhile" or "while that cooks"; the group already says they are simultaneous.
- Order the steps inside a group longest-first, so the step that must be started first is written first.
- Do not put every step in the recipe into one group. Most recipes have one or two real parallel blocks.
